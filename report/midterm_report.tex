\begin{reportbox}{Progress Summary}
The pipeline runs end to end. Phase 1 (the learned and classical
baselines) is done: Depth Anything V2 (DA-V2) is benchmarked
on all 654 NYU-Depth v2 test images at three model scales
(ViT-S/B/L) plus the metric indoor checkpoint, and the classical
depth-from-focus (DFF) baseline (modified Laplacian focus measure,
parabolic sub-frame interpolation, confidence map, all-focus guided
smoothing) runs on the rendered focal stacks and all 120 DDFF
12-Scene test stacks (Hazirbas et al.). Phase 2 (the focus-informed
metric refinement) is done on synthetic data: the robust scale fit and
regularized local correction run on six scenes split into tuning and
held-out sets, and the combined system runs qualitatively on real
DDFF stacks. Preliminary ablations on the tuning scenes cover
confidence weighting, the regularization and correction weights, and
focal-stack size (2/3/5/10 frames).
\end{reportbox}

\begin{reportbox}{Current Results}
NYU-Depth v2 test split (654 images, Eigen crop), DA-V2 across model
scales and alignment choices (Table~1):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Checkpoint & Alignment & AbsRel & RMSE (m) & $\delta_1$ \\
\midrule
ViT-S & median scale only & 0.494 & 2.349 & 0.393 \\
ViT-S & least squares & 0.062 & 0.297 & 0.956 \\
ViT-S & robust IRLS & 0.058 & 0.292 & 0.958 \\
ViT-B & least squares & 0.057 & 0.283 & 0.959 \\
ViT-L & least squares & \textbf{0.055} & \textbf{0.277} & \textbf{0.961} \\
Metric-Indoor-S & none (raw metric) & 0.218 & 0.650 & 0.670 \\
Metric-Indoor-S & least squares & 0.077 & 0.353 & 0.936 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[2pt]
\captext{Table~1. DA-V2 by checkpoint and alignment on the NYU-Depth
v2 Eigen test split; bold marks the column best.}
\end{center}
The affine fit matters: a median scale alone collapses accuracy
($\delta_1$ 0.39), so we model $q = s\hat d + t$ instead of a
scale-only alignment. Robust IRLS (iteratively reweighted least
squares) buys a small, consistent gain over plain least squares.
Bigger backbones help less than expected (S $\to$ L adds
$\approx 0.005$ $\delta_1$ at ${\approx}4\times$ the MPS runtime), so ViT-S
stays the project baseline. The metric indoor checkpoint has a real
scale prior but is biased on NYU (0.218 unaligned), and even aligned
it trails the relative models.

Synthetic focal stacks (10 frames, focus 0.9--7\,m, thin-lens blur
model). Regularization weights were tuned on two scenes (gallery,
stairs); Table~2 reports the held-out mean over the other four
(corridor, objects, slanted, shelves), metric depth scored without
alignment:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & AbsRel & RMSE (m) & $\delta_1$ \\
\midrule
DFF raw & 0.233 & 1.552 & 0.732 \\
DFF + all-focus smoothing & 0.221 & 1.510 & 0.702 \\
DA-V2 aligned (oracle) & 0.271 & 1.524 & 0.548 \\
DA-V2 + scale fit from focus & 0.308 & 1.614 & 0.459 \\
Refined (ours) & 0.259 & 1.483 & 0.575 \\
Combined (ours) & \textbf{0.206} & \textbf{1.284} & 0.704 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[2pt]
\captext{Table~2. Held-out mean over the four eval scenes; combined
falls back to the focus estimate on the rejected calibration
(slanted).}
\end{center}
The focus baseline is accurate where texture exists ($\delta_1$
0.89--1.00 on textured regions) and degrades on flat surfaces, exactly
where the confidence map reads low ($\bar w \approx
0.06$--0.26 textureless, 0.26--0.66 textured). All-focus guided
smoothing recovers part of that loss (textureless AbsRel 0.77 $\to$
0.61 on held-out scenes) but can smear confident edges into large flat
regions, a known limitation of normalized convolution. On the
calibrated held-out scenes (corridor, objects, shelves) the refinement
has the lowest AbsRel of any non-oracle method on every split: all
pixels 0.246 vs.\ raw 0.277, focus 0.266, scale fit 0.312; textured
0.211 vs.\ 0.224; textureless 0.281 vs.\ 0.307. Raw DFF keeps a higher
$\delta_1$ where texture exists (0.79 vs.\ 0.70) because its per-pixel
maxima stay sharp there. The one AbsRel it loses is low-texture
against oracle-aligned DA-V2 (0.281 vs.\ 0.265), and that fit uses
ground truth. The \emph{combined} system falls back to the focus
estimate on rejected calibrations; per scene it matches the better
component, and on the held-out mean it beats both (0.206 vs.\ 0.259
refined, 0.221 focus). DA-V2 underperforms the focus baseline on our
scenes even with oracle alignment, plausible given how far the
renderer sits from its training distribution, so we read this
benchmark as a stress test rather than a real-data statement.
Ablations on the tuning scenes: 2--3-frame stacks collapse the focus
estimate (AbsRel 10.5--114.9 vs.\ 0.12--0.24 at 5--10 frames), and
confidence weighting cuts both ways (refined AbsRel 0.188 vs.\ 0.210
uniform on stairs, but 0.164 vs.\ 0.114 on gallery).

\begin{reportbox}{Difficulties}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DDFF labels.} The public test archive ships 120 focal
  stacks but no ground truth (withheld for the benchmark server), and
  per-frame focus distances are not recorded. We reconstructed focus
  distances from the DDFF paper's refocus geometry (disparities linear
  in $[0.02,0.28]$~px, $z \approx 0.14/d$), verified the frame-to-depth
  ordering per stack by Spearman correlation against the learned
  prior (about a third of the stacks we checked prefer a reversed
  ordering), and limited DDFF to qualitative evaluation. Confidences on
  the real Lytro stacks run low ($\bar w \approx 0.02$--0.05); a
  per-scene confidence normalization in the fit gets calibration
  accepted on most stacks, but none of it can be scored without
  labels.
\item \textbf{NYU test split sourcing.} Both canonical sources were
  unreachable (the DenseDepth mirror returns 403 and the NYU host
  refuses connections); we used a mirror of the same 654-image Eigen
  test split and verified image count, units, and depth range.
\item \textbf{Defocus veiling.} In the layered renderer, blurred
  near-field texture bleeds over flat surfaces and lifts the focus
  measure there (the corridor's flat ceiling reads well above the
  textureless range).
  That is a genuine failure mode of the measurement, not a renderer
  artifact, and it caps how much we can trust $w$ at occlusion
  boundaries.
\item \textbf{Rejected fits.} Where the learned prediction
  anticorrelates with the focus estimate, the scale fit degenerates
  ($s \le 0$). On those scenes the refined output falls back to a
  constant plane at the confidence-weighted focus mean, marked
  uncalibrated, rather than emit bad metric depth.
\end{itemize}
\end{reportbox}

\begin{reportbox}{Changes from Proposal}
DDFF 12-Scene moved from a quantitative to a qualitative dataset: the
released test stacks carry no labels, as the proposal's planned
verification found. Rendered scenes now carry the quantitative
refinement evaluation, split into tuning and held-out sets so the
reported numbers are not tuned on the held-out scenes. The proposal
scheduled scale fitting and refinement for Oct 30--Nov 13; that stage
already runs on the synthetic scenes, roughly three weeks ahead.
Three additions beyond the proposed method: an all-focus guided
smoothing stage on the DFF baseline (normalized convolution in
inverse depth, weighted by confidence); a combined output that falls
back to the focus estimate on rejected calibrations (best held-out
mean, Table~2); and a degenerate-fit guard replacing silent bad
calibration.
\end{reportbox}
