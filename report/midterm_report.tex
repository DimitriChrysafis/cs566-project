\begin{reportbox}{Progress Summary}
The full pipeline is implemented and evaluated end to end. Phase 1 is
complete: DA-V2 is benchmarked on all 654 NYU-Depth v2 test images at
three model scales (ViT-S/B/L) plus the metric indoor checkpoint, and
the classical depth-from-focus baseline (modified Laplacian focus
measure, parabolic sub-frame interpolation, confidence map, all-focus
guided smoothing) runs on rendered focal stacks and all 120 DDFF test
stacks. Phase 2 is complete on synthetic data: robust scale fit and
regularized local correction run on six scenes with a tuning/held-out
split, and the combined system runs qualitatively on real DDFF stacks.
\end{reportbox}

\begin{reportbox}{Current Results}
NYU-Depth v2 test split (654 images, Eigen crop), DA-V2 across model
scales and alignment choices:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Checkpoint & Alignment & AbsRel & RMSE (m) & $\delta_1$ \\
\midrule
ViT-S & median scale only & 0.494 & 2.349 & 0.394 \\
ViT-S & least squares & 0.062 & 0.297 & 0.956 \\
ViT-S & robust IRLS & 0.058 & 0.292 & 0.958 \\
ViT-B & least squares & 0.057 & 0.283 & 0.959 \\
ViT-L & least squares & \textbf{0.055} & \textbf{0.277} & \textbf{0.961} \\
Metric-Indoor-S & none (raw metric) & 0.218 & 0.650 & 0.670 \\
Metric-Indoor-S & least squares & 0.077 & 0.353 & 0.936 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The affine fit matters: a median scale alone collapses accuracy
($\delta_1$ 0.39), which supports modeling $q = s\hat d + t$ rather
than rescaling alone. Robust IRLS buys a small consistent gain over
plain least squares. Scaling the backbone up helps less than expected
(S $\to$ L gains $\approx$0.005 $\delta_1$ at 4$\times$ the runtime on
MPS), so we keep ViT-S as the project baseline. The metric indoor
checkpoint carries a real scale prior but is biased on NYU (0.218
unaligned); aligned, it still trails the relative models.

Synthetic focal stacks (10 frames, focus 0.9--7\,m, thin-lens blur
model). Regularization weights were tuned on two scenes (gallery,
stairs); the table reports the held-out mean over the other four
(corridor, objects, slanted, shelves), metric depth scored without
alignment:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & AbsRel & RMSE (m) & $\delta_1$ \\
\midrule
DFF raw & 0.233 & 1.552 & 0.732 \\
DFF + all-focus smoothing & 0.221 & 1.510 & 0.702 \\
DA-V2 aligned (oracle) & 0.271 & 1.524 & 0.548 \\
DA-V2 + scale fit from focus & 0.308 & 1.614 & 0.459 \\
Refined (ours) & 0.259 & 1.483 & 0.575 \\
Combined (ours) & \textbf{0.206} & \textbf{1.284} & 0.704 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The focus baseline is accurate where texture exists
($\delta_1$ 0.89--1.00 on textured regions) but degrades on flat
regions, exactly where the confidence map reads low
($\bar w \approx 0.09$--0.25 textureless vs.\ 0.26--0.66 textured);
all-focus guided smoothing recovers part of that loss (textureless
AbsRel 0.77 $\to$ 0.62 on eval scenes) but can propagate confident
edges into large flat regions, a known limitation of normalized
convolution. On the calibrated evaluation scenes the refinement beats
every baseline in every split (AbsRel 0.246 vs.\ 0.266 focus, 0.312
scale fit; textured 0.211 vs.\ 0.224). The \emph{combined} system,
which falls back to the focus estimate when calibration is rejected,
strictly dominates both components. DA-V2 is weaker than the focus
baseline on our synthetic scenes even with oracle alignment ---
plausible, since the renderer is far from its training distribution ---
which makes this benchmark a useful stress test rather than a real-data
statement.
\end{reportbox}

\begin{reportbox}{Difficulties}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DDFF labels.} The public test archive ships 120 focal
  stacks but no ground truth (withheld for the benchmark server), and
  per-frame focus distances are not recorded. We reconstructed focus
  distances from the refocus geometry in the paper (disparities linear
  in $[0.02,0.28]$~px, $z \approx 0.14/d$), verified the frame-to-depth
  ordering per stack by Spearman correlation against the learned prior
  (about a third of the stacks we checked prefer reversed order), and
  limited DDFF to
  qualitative evaluation. Measured confidences on the real Lytro stacks
  are low ($\bar w \approx 0.01$--0.05); a per-scene confidence
  normalization added to the fit gets calibration accepted on most
  stacks, but the numbers cannot be scored without labels.
\item \textbf{NYU test split sourcing.} Both canonical sources were
  unreachable (the DenseDepth mirror returns 403 and the NYU host
  refuses connections); we used a mirror of the same 654-image Eigen
  test split and verified image count, units, and depth range.
\item \textbf{Defocus veiling.} In the layered renderer, blurred
  near-field texture bleeds over flat surfaces, which lifts the focus
  measure there (the synthetic corridor's flat ceiling reaches
  $w \approx 0.4$). This is a genuine failure mode of the focus
  measurement rather than a renderer artifact and it caps how far we
  can trust $w$ at occlusion boundaries.
\item \textbf{Rejected fits.} On scenes where the learned prediction
  anticorrelates with the focus estimate, the scale fit is degenerate
  ($s \le 0$); we fall back to the bounded uncalibrated output per the
  proposal rather than emit bad metric depth.
\end{itemize}
\end{reportbox}

\begin{reportbox}{Changes from Proposal}
DDFF 12-Scene moved from a quantitative to a qualitative dataset: the
verification the proposal made conditional showed the released test
stacks carry no labels. Rendered scenes now carry the quantitative
refinement evaluation, split into tuning and held-out sets so reported
numbers are not fit on the evaluation scenes. Three additions beyond
the proposed method: an all-focus guided smoothing stage on the DFF
baseline (normalized convolution in inverse depth, weighted by
confidence); a combined system output that uses the refined depth where
calibration succeeds and the focus estimate where it is rejected, which
strictly dominates both components on the held-out scenes; and a
degenerate-fit guard replacing silent bad calibration.
\end{reportbox}
