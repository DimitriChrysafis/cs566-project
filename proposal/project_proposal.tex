\begin{proposalbox}{The Problem}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.53\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
Take a photo of a chair. Nothing in the pixels says whether it is a real chair two meters away or a toy chair on a desk, because shrinking a scene and moving it closer leaves the image almost unchanged. That scale ambiguity is the core difficulty of monocular depth estimation. We want a dense depth map from one RGB image and, given a short focal stack, depth in real meters.

Modern networks sidestep scale. They predict relative depth, known only up to an affine map of inverse depth, $z = 1/(a\,d + b)$, and treat blur as noise to be robust against.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.44\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/blur_curve.pdf}

{\scriptsize\itshape Fig.~1. Thin-lens blur vs.\ depth for a 25\,mm f/2 lens. Blur is zero at the focus plane and grows on both sides, so a few focus settings pin down depth in meters.}
\end{minipage}

\smallskip
Blur is actually the one monocular cue with absolute scale. Under the thin-lens model, a point at depth $z$ spreads into a Gaussian with standard deviation
\[
\sigma(z) = k \left| 1 - \frac{z_f}{z} \right|, \qquad k = \frac{f_{\mathrm{px}}\, f}{2N\,(z_f - f)},
\]
where $f_{\mathrm{px}}$ is the focal length in pixels, $f$ the physical focal length, $N$ the f-number, and $z_f$ the focus distance. Everything on the right is a lens constant, so measured blur maps straight to meters (Fig.~1).

We care about this because depth is what turns a phone camera into a 3D sensor (portrait mode, AR, robot navigation), and because the project joins the two halves of this course: the optics of image formation and focus, and modern learned vision. It also comes with standard metrics, so gains are measurable rather than anecdotal.
\end{proposalbox}

\begin{proposalbox}{State of the Art}
Relative monocular depth is close to solved. MiDaS [1] and DPT [2] trained on mixed datasets with a scale-invariant loss; Depth Anything [3] scaled that recipe to 62M unlabeled images; Depth Anything V2 [4] trains on synthetic labels and ships 25M to 1.3B parameter models that run over 10$\times$ faster than the diffusion-based Marigold [5]. ZoeDepth [6] bolts on metric heads trained per domain, and Monodepth2 [7] is the standard self-supervised baseline. None of these uses defocus.

Depth from defocus (DFD) goes the other way. Pentland [8] and Subbarao and Surya [9] invert the thin-lens model from local blur, and DDFF [10] and Maximov et al.\ [11] learn the blur-to-depth mapping from focal stacks. Stereo methods such as SGM [12] use a second view instead.

\begin{center}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.29\textwidth}p{0.19\textwidth}p{0.44\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{Output} & \textbf{Where it fails} \\
\midrule
Learned monocular [1--7] & relative depth & metric scale; ZoeDepth [6] only within its training domain \\
Depth from defocus [8--11] & metric depth & textureless regions; needs a stack and calibration \\
Stereo matching [12] & metric depth & needs two calibrated views; textureless regions \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}
\end{center}
The first two rows fail in complementary places, and we have not found work that fuses a frozen zero-shot model with thin-lens blur at test time. That is our gap.
\end{proposalbox}

\begin{proposalbox}{Approach: Re-implement, Then Improve}
\textbf{Phase 1 (re-implementation).} Run Depth Anything V2 (ViT-S) [4] zero-shot on NYU-Depth v2 and DDFF-12, scored after least-squares scale-and-shift alignment as in [1]. Write classical depth from focus from scratch (modified Laplacian focus measure, per-pixel peak with sub-frame interpolation), scored on DDFF-12. SGM [12] is a stretch goal.

\textbf{Phase 2 (new).} Estimate per-pixel blur $\hat{\sigma}$ from the stack, then solve for metric depth as an affine rescale of the network's relative depth $\hat{d}$ plus a smooth residual $r$:
\[
z(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{s\,\hat{d}(\mathbf{x}) + t + r(\mathbf{x})}, \qquad
\min_{s,\,t,\,r}\; \sum_{\mathbf{x}} w(\mathbf{x})\, \rho\bigl(\sigma(z(\mathbf{x})) - \hat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\bigr) + \lambda \lVert \nabla r \rVert^2 .
\]
Here $\rho$ is a Huber loss and $w$ is the local focus-measure contrast, so pixels with no texture carry no weight and inherit shape from the network. The global $s, t$ fix scale; $r$ fixes local shape errors a single affine map cannot. It is a small per-image optimization with no retraining and no labels.

This should beat both parents because the parameterization matches what each source knows. The network's output is exactly an affine-invariant inverse depth, which is the family blur measurements can pin to metric scale, and the weight $w$ keeps unreliable blur out.
\end{proposalbox}

\begin{proposalbox}{Preliminary Result}
We already have a working depth-from-focus baseline. To test it before touching real data, we rendered a 10-frame focal stack (focus from 0.9 to 7 m) of a synthetic scene: a tilted city backdrop from 1.8 to 6 m, a painting at 1.1 m, and a plain grey card at 3 m, blurred layer by layer with the thin-lens model above.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/dff_preliminary.pdf}

{\scriptsize\itshape Fig.~2. Our depth-from-focus baseline on a rendered 10-frame stack. (a, b) two frames of the stack; (c) true depth; (d) our estimate; (e) relative error. Textures come from the HW1 images.}
\end{center}

On textured pixels, AbsRel is 0.085 with $\delta_{1.25} = 0.90$. On the textureless card it jumps to 0.52, since there is nothing for a focus measure to lock onto. That failure is precisely the region where a learned prior is reliable, which is the case for Phase 2.
\end{proposalbox}

\begin{proposalbox}{Evaluation and Plan}
\textbf{Data.} The 654-image NYU-Depth v2 test split [13]; DDFF 12-Scene [10] (real 10-frame Lytro focal stacks with depth ground truth; we evaluate on its labeled scenes); rendered stacks like Fig.~2 for controlled tests; our own phone focal stacks for qualitative results.

\textbf{Metrics.} AbsRel, SqRel, RMSE, RMSE-log, $\delta < 1.25, 1.25^2, 1.25^3$, plus metric scale error $\mathrm{mean}\,|\log(z_{\mathrm{pred}}/z_{\mathrm{gt}})|$, reported separately on textured and textureless pixels.

\textbf{Comparisons.} DA-V2 raw and oracle-aligned (an upper bound on what scale fitting can do), our DFF, and the fusion. Ablations remove the residual $r$, the weight $w$, and the smoothness term, and vary the frame count (2, 3, 5, 10). We also report runtime.
